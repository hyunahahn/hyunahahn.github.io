Introduction

How do we decide what/whom to talk about next?

  • Discourse coherence requires deciding what/whom to talk about next.
  • Verbs encode thematic roles.
  • Prior work:
    • Implicit causality: Who cased the event
    • Implicit consequentiality: Who is affected
    • Mostly on overt pronoun languages
  • Can Korean discourse coherence be explained within a similar framework?

Background

Theories on Discourse Coherence

  • Implicit Causality: Verbs guide causal attribution.
    • Crinean & Garnham (2006); Garvey et al. (1974); McKoon et al. (1993); Solstad & Bott (2022)
  • Implicit Consequentiality: Verbs guide consequence attribution.
    • Crinean & Garnham (2006); Garnham et al. (2021); Kim & Chun (2023); Stewart et al. (1998)
  • Proto-Roles: Thematic roles are largely either proto-agents or proto-patients.
    • Dowty (1991)
  • Thematic roles & discourse coherence: Thematic roles guide discourse continuation.
    • Stevenson et al. (2000); Stevenson et al. (1993)
  • Discourse Prediction: Can proto-patient prominence predict topic continuity?
    • Ahn (2025)

Implicit Causality

Garvey et al. (1974):

  • The prisoner confessed to the guard because he…
    • wanted to be released (N1) >> pressured him (N2)
    • N1 verbs: confess. join, sell, telephone, chase, approach
  • The mother punished her daughter because she…
    • discovered her guilt (N1) << admitted her guilt (N2)
    • N2 verbs: punish, criticize, praise, fear, blame
  • Verbs tend to attribute the cause of the event to one participant over the other.

Implicit Consequentiality

Crinean & Garnham (2006): Story-continuation

  • Because James dreaded Tom, …
    • he avoided Tom.
    • NP1 verbs: admire, adore, appreciate, like
  • Because John annoyed Sarah, …
    • she complained.
    • NP2 verbs: punish, thank, congraulate, scold
  • Verbs point to different NPs as the participant affected by the event.

Proto-Agents vs. Proto-Patients

Dowty (1991):

  • Agent vs. Patient: John hit Bill.
  • Source vs. Goal: John gave Bill a book.
  • Stimulus vs. Experiencer: John annoyed Bill.
  • Proto-agents:
    • Agents, sources, and stimulus
    • Thematic roles that cause events
  • Proto-patients:
    • Patients, goals, and experiencers
    • Thematic roles that are affected by events
  • Proto-agents tend to continue discourse.

Thematic Roles and Discourse Coherence

Stevenson et al. (1994)

  • Implicit causality and consequentiality differentiate thematic roles of verbs.
  • Thematic roles guide reference by making the participant linked to an event’s consequences most salient.
  • John hit Bill and he… \(\rightarrow\) “he” = Bill (patient)
  • Proto-Patient Bias

Topic Shift Prediction

Ahn (2025): Topic Shift and the thematic role of Overt NP

  1. Topic Shifted

    어제 저녁에 정미는 유나에게 폭력적인 남자 친구에 대해 털어놓았다.

    1. 유나가 (정미가) 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다. Experiencer
    2. (유나가) 정미가 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다. Stimulus

  2. Topic Continued

    어제 저녁에 정미는 유나의 폭력적인 남자 친구에 대해 알게 되었다.

    1. 정미가 (유나가) 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다. Experiencer
    2. (정미가) 유나가 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다. Stimulus
  • Topic Shift + Experiencer; Topic Continue + Stimulus == Acceptable
  • Topic Shift + Stimulus; Topic Continue + Experiencer == Unacceptable

Research Questions

  3. Is the proto-patient bias affected by the sentence structure: agent-first vs. patient-first?
  4. Does the proto-patient bias induce topic shift prediction?

Method

Participants

  • 25 native speakers of Korean
    • Composition: 20 university freshmen; 3 heritage Korean speakers; 2 older adults (in late 50s)
    • Gender: 11 male and 14 female
    • Compensation: Course credit

Stimuli

  • Same gender within items; minimal differences between conditions
  • 24 items with 2 conditions each; Latin-squared

  1. agent-patient: 강민이가 호영이를 때렸다. K hit H.

  2. patient-agent: 강민이가 호영이한테 맞았다. K got hit by H.

  3. source-goal: 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 보냈다.
    M sent a party invitation to J.

  4. goal-source: 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 받았다.
    M received a party invitation from J.

  5. stimulus-experiencer: 승준이가 재민이를 무섭게 한다. S frightens J.

  6. experiencer-stimulus: 승준이가 재민이를 무서워 한다. S fears J.

Experiment

  • Task: Story-continuation
  • Platform: Penn Controlled Internet-based experiment (PC IBEX)
  • Participants wrote a continuation for each prompt sentence
  • Duration: 20-30 minutes

Response Coding

  • 강민이가 호영이를 때렸다. K hit H.

    • 호영이는 참지 않고, 강민이를 한 대 더 때려주었다: patient
      H couldn’t hold back, and hit K back.

    • 강민이는 나빴다: agent
      K was mean.

  • 강민이가 호영이한테 맞았다.K got hit by H.

    • 강민이가 맞을 짓을 했다: patient
      K had it coming.

    • 호영이는 죄책감이 들었다: agent
      H felt guilty.

Response Coding (Continued)

  • 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 보냈다.
    M sent a party invitation to J

    • 준성이는 일이 있어서 파티에 가지 못했다: patient
      J had another commitment and couldn’t go to the party.
    • 초대장은 간결했다. 희고 질 좋은 종이에…: theme
      The invitation was simple. On fine white paper…

  • 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 받았다.
    M received a party invitation from J

    • 명준이는 파티에 가고 싶어 흥분된 마음을 감출 수가 없다: patient
      M was eager to attend the party and was unable to conceal his excitement.

Inaccurate Data

  • OTHERS
    • Thematic role reversion
      • 강민이가 호영이한테 맞았다. 그래서, 호영이가 화가 났다.
        K got hit by H. So, H is mad.
      • 동욱이가 준영이에게 잡혔다. 준영이가 도둑이고, 동욱이가 경찰이다.
        D got caught by J. J is the thief, and D is the police officer.
    • Unclear/ambiguous continuation
      • 동욱이가 준영이를 잡았다. 나도 알아. 그런 친구가 옆에 있는데 얼마나 다행이니?
        D caught J. I know, how lucky such a friend is beside Ø
      • 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 보냈다. 기쁘다.
        M sent a party invitation to J. Ø Happy.

Data Coding

  • Total number of responses: 25 participants x 24 items = 600 responses
    • 3 no responses
    • 122 “other” responses removed: inaccurate + unclear (20%)
    • Number of responses analyzed: 474
  • Data coding
    • 1: proto-patient
    • 0: agent, both (proto-agent + proto-patient), theme (dative verbs)

Statistical Analysis

  • Responses

    • If response starts with patient: 1; otherwise, 0

  • Logistic mixed effects modeling

    • participant and item intercepts only

    • Overall patient response model: patient ~ 1 + (1|participant) + (1|item)

    • Accuracy model: accuracy ~ 1+condition*type+(1|participant)+(1|item)

    • patient ratio model: patient~1+condition*type+(1|participant)+(1|item)

  • R version 4.6.0 (2026-04-24)

  • lmerTest

Discussion

Proto-Patient Bias in Korean

  • Patient-focused responses (64% patient vs. 36% non-patient)
  • NP order (condition): insignificant factor
  • Verb type: psych-verbs pattern differently
    • Fewer proto-patient responses when experiencer (proto-patient) comes first
  • Significant Interaction of condition and verb type
    • NP order significant only in psych-verbs

Is Topic Shift Predictable?

Ahn (2025)’s speculation:

  • Shorter reading times (RTs) when topic shift was expected
  • Topic shift cases: agent-first and patient-later

Current study:

  • Discourse continuation favors proto-patients
  • The proto-patient bias can indicate topic shift/continuation
  • Readers may unconsciously anticipate topic continuity or shift from thematic-role information.

Good-Enough Processing in Korean?

  • Ferreira and colleagues (Ferreira, 2003; Ferreira et al., 2003; Ferreira et al., 2009; Ferreira & Patson, 2007): Rather than accurately processing thematic roles, world knowledge aligned good enough processing
    • The man bit the dog.
    • The man was bitten by the dog.
    • The dog bit the man.
    • The dog was bitten by the man.

Good-Enough Processing in Korean?

  • Current study
    • More errors when patient is given first
    • Jane praised Jill.
    • Jane was praised by Jill.
    • With no world knowledge bias, agent-first sentences yielded higher accuracy.

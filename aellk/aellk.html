Predictive Discourse Processing in Korean

AELLK

2026-05-30

Introduction

How do we decide what/whom to talk about next?

  • Discourse coehrence requires what/whom to talk about next.
  • Verbs encode thematic roles.
  • Prior work:
    • Implicit causality: Who cased the event
    • Implicit consequentiality: Who is affected
    • Mostly on overt pronoun languages
  • Can Korean discourse coherence be explained within a similar framework?

Background

Theories on Discourse Coherence

  • Implicit Causality:
    • Crinean & Garnham (2006); Garvey et al. (1974); McKoon et al. (1993); Solstad & Bott (2022)
    • Verbs are biased to indicate the causer of the event, which affects pronoun interpretation.
  • Implicit Consequentiality:
    • Crinean & Garnham (2006); Garnham et al. (2021); Kim & Chun (2023)
    • Discourse flows in the direction in which consequences of events are focused on.
  • Thematic roles and discourse coherence:
    • Stevenson et al. (2000); Stevenson et al. (1993); Stewart et al. (1998)
    • Thematic roles that are affected by events tend to continue the discourse.

Implicit Causality

Garvey et al. (1974):

  • The prisoner confessed to the guard because he…
    • wanted to be released (N1) >> pressured him (N2)
  • The mother punished her daughter because she…
    • confessed her guilt (N1) << admitted her guilt (N2)

Implicit Consequentiality

Crinean & Garnham (2006):

  • John annoyed Bill, so he complained.
  • James hit Tom, so he cried.
  • Consequence falls on Bill and Tom.

Thematic Roles and Discourse Coherence

Stevenson et al. (1994)

  • Thematic roles guide reference by making the participant linked to an event’s consequences most salient.
  • John hit Bill and he… –> “he” = Bill (patient)
  • Proto-Patient Bias

Proto-Patient Bias

Proto-patients (Dowty, 1991)

  • Agent vs. Patient: John hit Bill.
  • Source vs. Goal: John gave Bill a book.
  • Stimulus vs. Experiencer: John annoyed Bill.
  • Patients, goals, and experiencers are thematic roles that are affected by events; thus, “Proto-patients”
  • Proto-patients may dominate discourse condinuation.

Topic Shift Prediction

Ahn (2025):

  1. Topic Shifted

    어제 저녁에 정미는 유나에게 폭력적인 남자 친구에 대해 털어놓았다.

    1. 유나가 (정미가) 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다.
    2. (유나가) 정미가 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다.

  2. Topic Continued

    어제 저녁에 정미는 유나의 폭력적인 남자 친구에 대해 알게 되었다.

    a. 정미가 (유나가) 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다.

    b. (정미가) 유나가 걱정스러웠는지 헤어지라고 충고했다.

  • RT: (1a) >> (2b) >> (1b) >> (2a)
  • Main effect of Topic Status
  • (1a): Agent-first + Patient-later = Topic Shift
  • Participants can predict topic shift

Research Questions

  1. Is Korean discourse coherence also affected by the proto-patient bias?
  2. Is the proto-patient bias observed across all proto-patient thematic roles?
  3. Is the proto-patient bias affected by the sentence structure: agent-first vs. patient-first?
  4. Does the proto-patient bias induce topic shift prediction?

Method

Participants

  • 25 native speakers of Korean
    • Composition: 20 university freshmen; 3 heritage Korean speakers; 2 older adults (in late 50s)
    • Gender: 11 male and 14 female
    • Compensation: Course credit

Stimuli

  • Same gender within items; minimal differences between conditions
  • 24 items with 2 conditions each; latin-squared

  1. agent-patient

    강민이가 호영이를 때렸다.

  2. patient-agent

    강민이가 호영이한테 맞았다.

  3. source-goal

    명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 보냈다.

  4. goal-source

    명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 받았다.

  5. stimulus-experiencer

    승준이가 재민이를 무섭게 한다.

  6. experiencer-stimulus

    승준이가 재민이를 무서워 한다.

Experiment

  • Task: Story-continuation
  • Platform: Penn Controlled Internet-based experiment (PC IBEX)
  • Participants wrote a continuation for each prompt sentence
  • Duration: 20-30 minutes

Response Coding

  • 강민이가 호영이를 때렸다.

    • 호영이는 참지 않고, 강민이를 한 대 더 때려주었다: patient

    • 강민이는 나빴다: agent

  • 강민이가 호영이한테 맞았다.

    • 강민이가 맞을 짓을 했다: patient

    • 호영이는 죄책감이 들었다: agent

  • 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 보냈다.

    • 준성이는 일이 있어서 파티에 가지 못했다: patient
    • 초대장은 간결했다. 희고 질 좋은 종이에…: theme

  • 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 받았다.

    • 명준이는 파티에 가고 싶어 흥분된 마음을 감출 수가 없다: patient

Inaccurate Data

  • OTHERS
    • Thematic role reversion
      • 강민이가 호영이한테 맞았다. 그래서, 호영이가 화가 났다.
      • 동욱이가 준영이에게 잡혔다. 준영이가 도둑이고, 동욱이가 경찰이다.
    • Unclear/ambiguous continuation
      • 동욱이가 준영이를 잡았다. 나도 알아. 그런 친구가 옆에 있는데 얼마나 다행이니?
      • 명준이가 준성이에게 파티 초대장을 보냈다. 기쁘다.

Data Coding

  • Total number of responses: 25 participants x 24 items = 600 responses
    • 3 no responses
    • 122 “other” responses removed: inaccurate + unclear (20%)
    • Number of responses analyzed: 474
  • Data coding
    • 1: patient
    • 0: agent, both (proto-agent + proto-patient), theme (psych-verbs)

Statistical Analysis

  • Responses

    • If response starts with patient: 1; otherwise, 0

  • Logistic mixed effects modeling

    • participant and item intercepts only

    • Overall patient response model: patient ~ 1 + (1|participant) + (1|item)

    • Accuracy model: accuracy ~ 1+condition*type+(1|participant)+(1|item)

    • patient ratio model: patient~1+condition*type+(1|participant)+(1|item)

  • R version 4.6.0 (2026-04-24)

  • lmerTest

Discussion

Proto-Patient Bias in Korean

  • Patient-focused responses (30% agent vs. 70% patient)
  • NP order (condition): insignificant factor
  • Verb type: psych-verbs pattern differently
    • Fewer patient responses when experiencer (proto-patient) comes first
  • Significant Interaction of condition and verb type
    • NP order significant only in psych-verbs

Is Topic Shift Predictable?

Ahn (2025)’s speculation:

  • Shorter reading times (RTs) when topic shift was expected
  • Topic shift cases: agent-first and patient-later

Current study:

  • Discourse continuation favors proto-patients
  • The proto-patient bias can indicate topic shift/continuation
  • Topic shift is guided by proto-patient bias

Good-Enough Processing in Korean?

  • Ferreira and colleagues (Ferreira, 2003; Ferreira et al., 2003; Ferreira et al., 2009; Ferreira & Patson, 2007): Rather than accurately processing thematic roles, world knowledge aligned good enough processing
    • The man bit the dog.
    • The man was bitten by the dog.
    • The dog bit the man.
    • The dog was bitten by the man.
  • Current study
    • More errors when patient is given first
    • Jane praised Jill.
    • Jane was praised by Jill.
    • With no world knowledge bias, agent-first sentences yielded higher accuracy.
